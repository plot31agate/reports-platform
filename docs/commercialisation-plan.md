Working notes from the sales/commercialisation planning session (Sept 2026).

## What we're commercialising

The full Digital Footprints platform as a complete **agency operating
system** covering the monthly client rhythm, not just a reporting tool:

- **Plan** — strategy + calendar
- **Make** — Studio (creative / social / content)
- **Push** — schedule + publish (via Buffer)
- **Report** — the reporting engine (live at reports.digital-footprints.co.uk)
- **Client HQ** — the client's front door / dashboard
- **Leads Central** — WordPress lead scoring, follow-up, CRM connect

Positioning line: *"Your whole marketing month, run in one place — planned,
made, published and proven."*

## The pivotal open decision

**Does the customer log in and run it themselves (self-serve), or does DF
operate it for them (managed)?** This swings build cost, price and target
buyer more than anything else. Resolve this before finalising pricing.

- **DF-operated (managed):** ~zero further build; sell outcomes; revenue
  scales with team capacity.
- **Customer-operated (self-serve):** needs whatever multi-tenant signup,
  billing, roles and white-label depth aren't already in place.

## The model we're leading with — paid setup → ongoing licence

Chosen spearhead (the "Launchpad → licence" model):

- **The Launchpad (paid setup):** fixed-fee 2–4 week engagement. Define
  positioning, strategy and creative direction; configure Plan/Make/Push/
  Report + Client HQ; connect data + channels (Buffer, GA4, Search Console,
  Ahrefs, Meta); stand up Leads Central; train the client. Illustrative
  **£3–10k**. This is the low-commitment paid "yes" that de-risks the bigger
  sale — and where the platform sells the licence for you.
- **The licence (ongoing):** three shapes off the same platform —
  - Self-serve licence (they run it): illustrative **£300–900/mo**
  - Managed (DF runs the cycle — retainer, re-platformed): **£1.5–5k/mo**
  - Hybrid (they create; DF plans + reports + QA): in between
- **Leads Central:** add-on **£49–199/mo per site**, or the cheap front door
  into the platform.

Maps onto the Prove It / Plan & Prove / Full Cycle tiers in the sales brief.
Pricing is illustrative, not set — anchor to retainer displacement / value.

**Why this model:** with the full cycle built, the platform *is* the
recurring value (client is in it every month), which fixes the classic
setup-then-licence failure where the licence has nothing to renew for.

## Routes reviewed (honest summary)

| Route | Fit | Build to sell | Moat impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Managed productised service (setup → managed licence) | Strongest, lowest-risk | ~£0 build; senior time per setup | Neutral / protects |
| Self-serve SaaS licence | Strong *if* multi-tenant/billing exist | Low if they exist; large if not | Erodes if sold to peers |
| White-label to agencies | Strong B2B2B (it's an agency OS) | Medium (partner layer, deep white-label) | Arms competitors |
| Leads Central standalone wedge | Clean entry product | Low (packaging + distribution) | Neutral |
| Keep internal | Baseline | £0 | Protects |

## Target market / who could use it

- **Primary:** ambitious SMB / mid-market brands in competitive or regulated
  sectors (Sportingtech / iGaming as the proof case), marketing leads who
  must prove ROI to a board. UK-first, starting in the proven vertical.
- **White-label:** small / solo agencies who'd never build this themselves.
- **Leads Central:** any WordPress business with a sales team and leads
  dying in a shared inbox.

## Plan — sequence it, gated on evidence

1. **Now:** launch the managed productised service (Launchpad → managed
   licence) to 3–5 warm relationships. Templatise the Launchpad from day one
   so it doesn't become bespoke consulting.
2. **In parallel, cheap:** put Leads Central out as the low-cost front door /
   lead magnet feeding Launchpad enquiries.
3. **If managed retention holds ~3 months:** open a self-serve tier (if
   already multi-tenant), else decide whether that build is worth funding on
   the demand now proven.
4. **Only on proven pull:** pilot white-label with a single non-competing
   agency (different sector / geography), rev-share not flat fee.

Two numbers that matter early: **Launchpad → managed-licence conversion
rate**, and **3-month retention**. Nothing else.

## Marketing approach (for the spearhead)

- **Wedge offer:** "Book a Launchpad" — paid, low-commitment strategy +
  creative + setup sprint. Easier to sell than a 12-month platform contract.
- **Hero proof:** a single end-to-end demo — plan a month, make a piece of
  content, push it live via Buffer, then show the report that measures it.
  A ~3-minute Loom of the full loop beats any deck, and reporting-only
  rivals can't show it.
- **Assets we have:** live report, sales brief, Sportingtech case study.
  To make: the end-to-end demo video and a one-page Launchpad sheet.
- **Channels:** warm network + referrals first; LinkedIn thought-leadership
  from DF principals on running + proving the whole marketing month; one
  amplified case study; tight outbound to a defined ICP. Hold paid
  acquisition until Launchpad → licence conversion is proven.

## Pre-mortem — the failure modes to watch

1. **Bespoke Launchpads destroy margin** (#1 risk) — templatise per vertical
   or it's consulting with a login.
2. **Marketed as software, not the outcome** — buyers want to stop losing
   budget arguments and stop juggling five tools; lead there.
3. **Skipping the end-to-end demo** — the full-cycle loop is the only real
   differentiator; feature-led marketing makes us look like any dashboard.
4. **Opening self-serve / white-label before proving managed demand** —
   funds a build + support burden on hope, and (white-label) arms competitors.
5. **Leads Central mistaken for a business** — it's a front door, not a P&L.
